# Editing Standard

Use these rule IDs in the revision table. Apply only rules relevant to the passage.

## Accuracy and Meaning

- **G1 — Mechanical accuracy:** Check spelling, typography, punctuation, missing or duplicated words, agreement, tense, articles, units, symbols, figure/table callouts, and malformed characters sentence by sentence. Then inspect every substantive phrase for grammar, natural collocation, and exact meaning; an understandable sentence can still contain an unacceptable local construction.
- **G2 — Preserve meaning and stance:** Do not change the study design, direction or magnitude of an effect, biological entity, comparison, time point, dose, sample size, statistical result, or the author's intended rhetorical strength without evidence and author confirmation. Do not systematically downgrade claims merely because a more cautious formulation is possible.
- **G3 — Evidence strength without defensive prose:** Match language to the design. Association does not establish causation; expression data alone do not establish pathway activation, function, therapeutic efficacy, or mechanism. When a claim is arguable and supported by the overall evidence chain, preserve its strength. Do not routinely add a conservative alternative; provide claim-calibration options only when the user requests them or when ambiguity materially affects scientific accuracy. Revise the passage itself only for a clear design-to-claim error.
- **G4 — Concrete scientific detail:** Replace vague timing, population, or setting language with exact information only when supplied or supported by a source. Otherwise flag the missing detail; never fabricate specificity.
- **G4a — Missing statistics:** Never invent statistical support or call a result significant without a supporting test. If a direction is visible or supplied but the test is missing, write a direct descriptive result and flag the missing statistic outside the passage. Do not insert stock caveats into Results.

## Logic and Readability

- **G5 — Result-first information focus:** First identify the one result the reader should remember. Put the biological entity, comparison, or main finding in the grammatical subject or opening clause when this produces natural English. Avoid `analysis showed`, `we performed`, and `to determine` when the result can begin directly, but do not force an awkward passive construction merely to demote the method.
- **G5a — Figure-level logic before sentence-level style:** In multi-panel Results, determine the overall figure question, the role of each panel, and the dependency between adjacent panels before rewriting individual sentences. Result-first style must not turn panels into disconnected observations.
- **G5b — Purposeful bridges:** Preserve or add one concise bridge when a new panel tests a property not established by the previous panel, such as specificity, independence, mechanism, persistence, mediation, or external validity. The bridge should name the unresolved question (`To determine whether the signature was specific to gout...`), not merely announce activity (`We next performed another analysis...`).
- **G5c — Minimal bridge wording:** Name only the highest-level unresolved property needed to orient the reader. Do not restate its logical opposite or enumerate alternatives already evident from the comparison groups. Prefer `To determine whether the signature was specific to gout...` over `To determine whether the signature was specific to gout rather than attributable to hyperuricemia or general AMI susceptibility...`.
- **G5d — Focus without grammatical distortion:** Choose sentence structure by information focus, not by a blanket ban on method-led syntax. Prefer `A 19-protein AMI signature was identified from the 115 candidates using L1-penalized Cox regression` when the signature is the point. Reject `A 19-protein signature was further discovered among 115 proteins by using...`, because `further discovered among` and `by using` weaken both naturalness and precision. Use `identified`, `selected`, `derived`, or `retained` according to what the model actually did; do not use `discovered` merely to sound more innovative.
- **G6 — English logic:** Make parallel, sequential, causal, explanatory, inferential, and contrastive relations explicit where needed. Prefer a defensible chain of evidence over a list of loosely related observations. Remove redundant transitions and repeated conclusions.
- **G6a — Connector logic:** Treat connectors as statements about the relationship between propositions. Use `thus` or `therefore` only when the preceding evidence directly supports or logically yields the following conclusion; never add them as generic markers of brevity or emphasis. Use `These findings indicate` for a direct evidence-to-interpretation step, `These findings suggest` for a plausible but non-exclusive interpretation, `consistently` for concordant independent evidence, and `may explain` when a later result offers a reason for an earlier finding. Use no connector when sentence order already makes the relation clear. The sentence `Thus, the 19-protein score identified a substantial subset of AMI cases missed by CRP and IL-1β` is appropriate after overlap data directly show that result.
- **G6b — Positive scientific motivation:** In Results, motivate the next analysis through the scientific opportunity created by the preceding result or the additional capability of the new design. Do not write bridges led by `because the previous dataset lacked`, `owing to insufficient data`, `to overcome the limitation`, or equivalent deficit framing. Prefer `The independent real-world cohort also enabled us to assess the relationship between flare burden and the 19-protein signature.` Report necessary limitations separately in the appropriate Discussion, Methods, or Editorial notes; never use them as the narrative engine of Results.
- **G6c — Evidence-derived bridge:** A transition should connect two evidence nodes, not merely announce chronology. Ask what the preceding finding made scientifically important to test next. For example, after establishing the prognostic value of a protein signature, `A prerequisite for such a mechanistic role is that the protein is persistently and highly expressed in the context of gout inflammation` creates the premise for examining the expression patterns and cellular sources of the genes encoding the signature proteins.
- **G7 — Concision:** Preserve information while shortening long noun strings, repeated qualifiers, duplicate methods, redundant transitions, and repeated conclusions. On polishing tasks, perform a final compression pass and prefer the shortest conventional sentence that preserves the intended meaning and emphasis. Split or merge according to logic, not a fixed length rule.
- **G7a — One-sentence sufficiency:** If one sentence can communicate the result and its necessary inference, use one sentence. Do not distribute one point across a purpose sentence, a result sentence, a paraphrased interpretation, and a concluding restatement. Every additional sentence must contribute new evidence, a necessary contrast, or a distinct inference.
- **G7b — No delayed result:** Put the central entity, comparison, or quantitative result before secondary procedural detail. Remove generic scene-setting such as `to further explore` or retrospective experiment summaries only when panel order and the preceding result already make the purpose clear. Retain a brief `To determine whether...` or equivalent bridge when it supplies the otherwise missing reason for the next analysis.
- **G7c — No same-level semantic echo:** Delete paragraph-ending formulations such as `These findings indicate`, `Taken together`, or `This result is consistent with` only when they repeat the preceding observation at the same conceptual level. Keep a summary that converts numbers into the central innovation, comparative advantage, mechanism, or explanation.
- **G7d — Selective detail:** Results should carry the data needed for the main argument, not reproduce every number visible in a figure or table. Preserve decisive effect sizes, uncertainty, significance, and comparisons; compress secondary values and routine methods when their omission does not change interpretation.
- **G7e — Logic-preserving compression:** Delete lexical repetition, duplicated interpretation, and routine procedural detail before deleting motivation or transitions. Compression is successful only when the revised passage is shorter and the panel-to-panel argument remains fully recoverable.
- **G7f — No orphan analyses:** If a reader can understand what an analysis found but not why it was performed, the prose is under-explained. Add the shortest scientifically meaningful bridge linking the prior finding to the new question.
- **G7g — No entailed repetition:** Delete a statement when another statement in the same sentence necessarily conveys it. For example, `RET was the only inverse component` already conveys that the other 18 coefficients were positive; report both only when the number 18 carries separate analytical importance.
- **G7h — Answer-bearing transition:** When a panel explains a preceding pattern, combine the bridge, analytical basis, and answer where possible without coining a label for that pattern. Prefer `To distinguish the urate- and gout-related components of the signature, the proteins were classified according to continuous urate–protein associations and urate-adjusted differences between gout and hyperuricemia...` over `To explain the limited cross-phenotype replication...`. Avoid a separate question sentence that previews the same categories reported in the conclusion.
- **G7i — Semantic-lift test:** Before deleting a summary sentence, compare its function with the preceding sentence. If the preceding sentence reports observations and the summary states why they matter to the paper, the summary adds a semantic level and should remain. Example: after reporting cases detected despite low CRP or IL-1β, retain `Thus, the 19-protein score identified a substantial subset of AMI cases missed by CRP and IL-1β.`
- **G7j — Evidence-module closure:** Identify distinct evidence modules within a figure and give each central module its own concise closure. Example: panels showing incremental case capture support the marker's comparative advantage; a subsequent survival panel supports long-term prognostic relevance. Do not treat the second conclusion as a replacement for the first.
- **G7k — Cross-module explanation:** If the second module can explain the first, add a separate synthesis sentence rather than forcing the relationship to remain implicit. For example, after showing both greater case identification and early, persistent separation of survival curves, write `This may explain why the 19-protein score showed greater long-term sensitivity than conventional inflammatory markers.` Preserve an author's clear integrative term such as `long-term sensitivity` when it accurately communicates the combined implication; do not automatically dilute it into another recital of the case counts.
- **G8 — Clear reference:** Ensure `it`, `they`, `this`, `these`, and similar expressions have one unmistakable antecedent. Prefer the entity name when ambiguity is possible.
- **G9 — Sentence variety with precision:** Vary structure when repetition distracts, including overuse of `suggesting that`, `indicating that`, infinitive `to`, or the same transition. Do not force synonyms, compressed labels, or unusual collocations merely to avoid repetition.

## Natural Biomedical Style

- **G10 — Plain professional wording:** Prefer familiar, precise verbs and concrete observations. Be cautious with dense use of terms such as `elucidate`, `unravel`, `shed light on`, `underscore`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `intricate`, `multifaceted`, `robust`, `notably`, `remarkably`, `collectively`, `novel insights`, `underlying mechanism`, and `potential therapeutic target`.
- These expressions are not forbidden. Retain one when it is conventional, accurate, and proportionate to the evidence. The goal is not mechanical synonym substitution.
- Delete empty evaluative openers such as `Interestingly` or `Importantly` when the result itself carries the emphasis.
- Prefer specific descriptions (`was higher`, exact effect and *P* value) over unqualified `significantly`, `markedly`, or `substantially`. Use `significant` only for statistical significance unless context clearly indicates otherwise.
- Avoid repetitive connector templates. Often the logical order of sentences makes `furthermore`, `moreover`, `therefore`, or `taken together` unnecessary.
- Do not add reviewer-facing limitations or self-protective qualifications to Results as stylistic filler. Keep them in Editorial notes unless they are necessary to prevent a false statement.
- **G10a — Direction before abstraction:** When the result has a known direction, name it directly. Prefer `remained elevated during the period before AMI and increased further as AMI approached` to `remained abnormal` or `defined a proteomic state that intensified`. Use `state`, `signal`, `profile`, `landscape`, `coverage`, or `alteration` only when the noun has a clear referent and adds a useful level of interpretation.
- **G10b — Qualifier necessity test:** Retain `clinical`, `cohort-level`, `individual-level`, `persistent`, and similar qualifiers only when they distinguish one state, analytical level, or time frame from another. `At the cohort level` is useful when explicitly contrasted with within-individual analysis; otherwise `The mean trajectory...` is clearer. `Clinical onset` is useful when distinguished from subclinical onset; otherwise `AMI onset` is sufficient.
- **G10c — Time-course clarity:** State the time window, direction, and event relation separately enough to remain interpretable. Use `throughout the 15-year period before AMI` only if elevation was sustained across the full interval; use `during much of the 15-year period before AMI` when that is what the data show. A clear model is: `The five gout-specific proteins remained elevated during much of the 15-year period before AMI and increased further as AMI approached, indicating a sustained gout-associated proteomic alteration that became more pronounced before AMI.`
- **G10d — Strongest defensible wording:** Evaluate titles and take-home sentences against the complete evidence chain, not one panel in isolation, and use the strongest scientifically defensible formulation. Preserve supported terms such as `specific`, `superior`, `most prominent`, `persistent`, `improved`, `drives`, or `mediates`; do not replace them with flatter descriptions merely because a more conservative alternative exists. Downgrade only for a clear evidence-to-claim mismatch.
- **G10e — Title grammar and force:** A strong title must still use the correct scientific noun and natural syntax. Prefer `Plasma proteomic profiling identifies...` or `Plasma proteomics identifies...` to `Plasma proteomic identifies...`. When comparative evidence supports specificity, retain `a 19-protein signature specific to incident AMI in people with gout` rather than weakening the title to `a signature identified in people with gout` without a scientific reason.
- **G10f — Author revisions reveal intent, not automatic copy:** Treat the author's edits as decisive evidence of the desired focus, logic, and rhetorical strength, while still correcting local grammar, agreement, articles, and collocation. For example, revise `After discovery of the 19-protein signature emerged several years before AMI in gout patients` to `After showing that the 19-protein signature emerged several years before AMI in people with gout...`; revise `an proteomic component`, `over of the 15-year period`, `the cohort enable`, and `sensitivity ... were` rather than preserving them verbatim.

## Terminology and Nomenclature

- **G11 — Verified terminology:** Confirm specialized names, diseases, assays, cell types, drugs, biomarkers, and preferred field expressions using authoritative nomenclature sources, reporting guidelines, the target journal, or strong recent papers. Provide the supporting link or citation; do not claim verification without a source.
- **G12 — Genes and proteins:** Apply the current official nomenclature for the stated species and verify the symbol. In standard prose, human gene symbols are generally uppercase and italicized (for example, *SPP1*); mouse gene symbols generally use initial uppercase followed by lowercase and are italicized (for example, *Mmp9*). Protein symbols/names are not italicized. Explicitly distinguish gene expression, transcript abundance, and protein level when both gene and protein products appear. Follow a target journal's documented house style if it differs.
- **G13 — Abbreviations:** Define each abbreviation at first use in the relevant independently read unit, then use it consistently. Do not define universally recognized units or symbols unnecessarily. Check the abstract and figure legends separately when journal readers may encounter them independently.

## Citation Check

- Mark externally verifiable background, prevalence, threshold, mechanism, guideline, and prior-work claims that lack support.
- Do not require references for the authors' own reported results, self-evident transitions, or ordinary methods details unless attribution is needed.
- Recommend up to three directly relevant references per sentence. Prefer primary studies for specific findings and authoritative guidelines or high-quality reviews for broad definitions.
- Never preserve a citation merely because it is present: verify that it supports the exact claim when citation checking is requested or when the mismatch is apparent.
