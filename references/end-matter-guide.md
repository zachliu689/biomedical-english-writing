# End-matter Guide

Read this file only when drafting or polishing Acknowledgements, Author contributions, Competing interests, Funding, Patient consent, or Ethics approval. Do not invent names, roles, grant details, approvals, consent scope, or disclosures.

## Acknowledgements

Request, when missing:

- full official names of companies, facilities, research teams, or individuals;
- the exact support provided, such as an assay, animal supply, sample processing, language editing, or data access;
- public dataset accession numbers and the original studies that must also be cited;
- whether the support was paid, collaborative, or provided in kind when the target journal requires disclosure.

Write one concise paragraph. Use concrete contributions (`technical support with plasma proteomic profiling`, `supplied the mice`) rather than generic gratitude. Do not place authorship-level intellectual contributions in Acknowledgements.

## Author contributions

Request:

- final author order and unambiguous initials;
- equal-contribution, co-corresponding, senior-author, and guarantor designations;
- each author's approximate contributions;
- confirmation that all authors approved the final manuscript and accept accountability when required.

Use the target journal's format or the CRediT taxonomy. Equal first authorship does not justify invented or inflated roles; assign substantial roles only when the user confirms them. Keep the statement concise and avoid repeating the same role in multiple phrasings.

## Competing interests

Ask whether any financial or non-financial competing interests exist. When the user confirms none, use: `The authors declare no competing interests.` Do not assume absence of conflicts merely because none were supplied.

## Funding

Request:

- each funder's official full name;
- grant or award number;
- recipient author or initials;
- any mandatory funder wording or role statement.

Verify official English funder names when possible. Preserve exact grant numbers and distinguish Funding from Competing interests.

## Patient consent

Clarify whether the statement concerns consent to participate, consent for publication, or both. Request whether consent was written or oral, who provided it, and whether a waiver was granted. Do not convert participation consent into publication consent.

Common forms, used only when accurate:

- `Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.`
- `Consent for publication was obtained from all participants.`
- `Consent for publication was not applicable because no identifiable participant information is reported.`

## Ethics approval

Request:

- the full ethics committee or institutional review board name;
- institution;
- approval number;
- whether separate human and animal approvals apply;
- any required declaration, such as adherence to the Declaration of Helsinki or institutional animal-care guidelines.

State only confirmed approvals. Use `approval no.` consistently and do not add ethical frameworks unless the user or approval documentation confirms them.

## Output

Return submission-ready sections first. Follow with a short list of missing confirmations only when necessary. Do not bury the declarations in a sentence-level revision table unless the user asks for tracked editing.
